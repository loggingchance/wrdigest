Market Opportunities

Northeast — New York

Fort Drum puts 312 acres of standing timber to bid today

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers closes bids today on six standing-timber harvest areas totaling 312 acres at Fort Drum. Timber is sold by harvested weight, with sawlogs paid on the Fort Drum sliding scale; the notice does not require chipping.

Most cutting runs from October 15, 2026 through spring 2027, while two items extend into a second cutting season through April 15, 2028. That gives northern New York contractors a sizable winter-oriented federal harvest opportunity with different operating windows across the six items.

Southeast — Arkansas / Mississippi

Southern pine thinning bids bunch up today

Three Arkansas plantation-thinning sales close at 2 p.m. today: about 45 acres in Miller County, 94 acres in Little River County and 193 acres across three tracts in Ouachita County. The Miller County second thin targets 70–80 square feet of residual basal area; the Little River and Ouachita first thins use fifth-row removal plus operator selection to the same residual range.

Separately, Southeast Forestlands closes a roughly 120-acre Newton County, Mississippi lump-sum sale at 10 a.m. Central today. The cluster provides a useful near-term read on contractor and buyer appetite for smaller southern thinning and harvest jobs.

Forestry & Timber

Canada — British Columbia

Two B.C. community forests gain more than 28,100 m³ of annual fibre

British Columbia is inviting the McLeod Lake Mackenzie Community Forest to expand by almost 10,400 hectares, carrying 20,000 cubic metres of annual fibre. The North Island Community Forest is advancing an expansion with more than 8,100 cubic metres annually.

The practical significance is local control of fibre: the province explicitly ties the expansions to local manufacturing, jobs and forest management. B.C. now has 62 community forests, making these additions part of a broader tenure lane rather than isolated pilot projects.

Markets & Pricing

North America

Canadian Forest Service refreshes lumber, panel and pulp benchmarks

Natural Resources Canada refreshed its public forest-product price page September 16. It consolidates weekly North American softwood lumber and panel prices, monthly pulp prices for the United States, Europe and China, and exchange rates for Canada's major trading partners.

The value is consistency rather than granularity: it is a government-maintained benchmark page that can be checked weekly without a paid market-data subscription.

Under the Radar

Inland West — Idaho / Montana

Two state timber auctions hit the calendar today

Idaho Department of Lands opens Mica Bay GNA in Kootenai County today: an estimated 7,923 tons with a three-year term and an average minimum bid of $14.67 per ton. Montana DNRC also lists Baldy Buzzcut for a September 17 bid opening.

The Idaho sale is especially useful as a small-volume price point alongside the much larger MBF sales moving through the region this month. Watching whether smaller GNA material attracts competition can say something different about contractor capacity and marginal haul economics than headline sawlog auctions.

Other Significant Developments

FBS business lens — lumber markets

Discipline holds as affordability tightens

RedBook Lumber Data's newly published Q3 outlook describes a lumber market where housing affordability remains the constraint and operating discipline is the response. That fits the current capacity picture: mills can add machinery and nameplate output faster than end-market demand improves.

The useful business lens is straightforward—throughput is valuable only when it produces contribution margin. In a constrained market, inventory, purchasing, utilization and product mix can matter more than chasing maximum gross production.
