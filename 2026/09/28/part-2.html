Fifty-year Maine study finds whole-tree harvesting changed composition more than productivity

A Forest Service summary of work at Maine's Penobscot Experimental Forest compares whole-tree harvesting, stem-only harvesting, prescribed burning and unharvested conditions in a study begun in 1964 and repeated in 2018.

After 50 years, researchers found no consistent differences in stand stocking or structure between whole-tree and stem-only treatments, and no consistent long-term nutrient differences attributable to the harvest treatments. Species composition did shift. The result adds useful nuance to residue-removal decisions: site geology, hydrology and treatment design may matter more to long-term productivity than a blanket assumption that whole-tree harvesting necessarily creates a persistent penalty.
