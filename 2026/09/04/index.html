Daily edition

September 4, 2026

Prepared from public sources. Links lead to original material.

What matters this morning

  • U.S. Forest Service research footprint is being redrawn: 23 research facilities will close, while 41 previously slated for closure will stay open. The agency says the consolidation will save about $16 million and that ongoing science will continue elsewhere.
  • Northeastern Ontario could regain a major fiber sink. Bioveld North is spending $10.5 million on engineering for a possible restart of the Espanola pulp mill; Ontario is supplying $5.1 million. A restart could consume up to 1.8 million m³ of wood annually.
  • J.D. Irving has fresh gatewood pricing posted for Sept. 4 across New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Maine procurement areas, with current prices by mill, product and trucking zone.
  • Minnesota expects its best red-pine cone crop in about a decade and is paying $95/bushel for clean ripe closed cones, targeting 1,000 bushels to support future nursery production.
  • The National Forest Foundation is seeking a contractor for roughly 870 acres of Hurricane Helene fuel-reduction work on Pisgah National Forest, including removal of sawtimber and pulpwood.

Forestry & Timber

Lake States

Minnesota raises red-pine cone price to $95/bushel

Minnesota DNR expects a bumper red-pine cone crop, roughly a once-in-a-decade event, and wants to purchase about 1,000 bushels. The seed will feed the State Forest Nursery at Akeley; DNR says it replants roughly 2,000 acres of red pine each year using nearly 1 million seedlings. Nursery capacity starts several years before a seedling reaches the woods.

Pacific Northwest

Logging restrictions ease in northwest Oregon

Oregon DNR lowered fire danger to “moderate” and moved industrial operations to IFPL 1 across its NW-1, NW-2 and NW-3 zones, including the Tillamook State Forest, effective Sept. 3. IFPL 1 is the lowest industrial precaution level, easing restrictions on logging and other woods work.

Midwest

Shawnee timber project halted by federal court

A federal judge ordered the Forest Service to halt a roughly 70-acre oak/hickory logging project in Shawnee National Forest after finding the agency approved it before obtaining a final biological opinion under the Endangered Species Act. About 40 acres had already been logged before an earlier restraining order.

Southeast

Great Southern Wood adding 17th treating plant in North Carolina

Great Southern Wood-NC plans a $40 million pressure-treated lumber facility at Laurinburg-Maxton Airport in Scotland County, creating 43 jobs. North Carolina Railroad is contributing up to $600,000 for on-site rail infrastructure; the facility is expected to receive and distribute at least 421 railcars annually by 2030.

Ontario

$17.3 million targets sawmill modernization and biomass use

Ontario is putting more than $17.3 million into four northern forest-sector projects. Interfor gets more than $9.6 million for an Elk Lake modernization that includes biomass heat and is projected to raise production 27%, cut natural-gas use 80% and reduce annual operating costs by nearly $3 million. GreenFirst gets $5.9 million for a Chapleau production line expected to improve productivity 24%, plus $1.6 million to study a biofuel facility; Atlantic Power gets $180,000 for controls at the Calstock power plant.

North America

Canada gains share of U.S. softwood imports

Lesprom Analytics reports that U.S. softwood lumber imports totaled 7.2 million m³ in Q2 2026 and Canada gained six percentage points of U.S. import share while Germany lost three. At the same time, Canada’s first-half shipments across the ten largest global import markets were 1.8 million m³ lower than a year earlier.

Pulp, Paper & Biomass

Ontario

Espanola restart study could restore 1.8 million m³/year of wood demand

Bioveld North, part of BMI Group, has begun a $10.5 million front-end engineering program for the idled Espanola pulp and paper mill, with Ontario contributing $5.1 million and Bioveld $5.4 million. The concept under study would produce specialty food-grade kraft paper and could create about 400 direct jobs and 2,000 indirect jobs. Ontario says a restart could use up to 1.8 million m³ of sustainably sourced wood annually.

Under the Radar

Mountain West

Raton, New Mexico gets a biomass-to-biocarbon production facility

Carbogenics USA has secured its first U.S. manufacturing site in Raton. The operation is intended to convert locally sourced low-value biomass into engineered biocarbons for anaerobic digestion, water treatment, carbon removal and environmental remediation; the company says initial CreChar production should begin in the coming weeks.

Market Opportunities

Appalachia

870-acre Pisgah fuel-reduction contract due Sept. 11

The National Forest Foundation is soliciting a contractor for the Shinbone Fuel Reduction project on roughly 870 acres in the Appalachian Ranger District of Pisgah National Forest. Work includes removal of blown-over, broken, root-sprung and leaning trees resulting from Hurricane Helene, including sawtimber and pulpwood, plus erosion control, water-barring, slashing, vegetation establishment and road maintenance.

Government / Research Infrastructure

National

Forest Service to close 23 research facilities; 41 spared

The Forest Service finalized a facilities review that will close 23 research and development locations in more than a dozen states while retaining 41 sites that had previously been targeted for closure. Ninety-five employees are expected to shift to other sites within commuting areas. Combined with previously announced regional-office closures, the agency estimates roughly $16 million in savings.

Opportunities worth watching

  • Espanola’s engineering work deserves close attention. A credible path to 1.8 million m³/year of renewed wood consumption would have regional effects well before restart.
  • The Pisgah Shinbone solicitation is a good example of post-disturbance work where logging, fuel reduction and restoration money overlap. Similar Helene-related contracting opportunities may be worth actively tracking across the southern Appalachians.
  • Carbogenics is small, but Raton is the kind of project to watch for evidence that low-value wildfire biomass can support specialized carbon products at commercial scale.
