Forestry & Timber

Oregon — State Forests

Western state forests gain the first federal permit under Oregon’s HCP

Oregon Department of Forestry received an incidental-take permit from NOAA Fisheries on September 11 for its Western State Forests Habitat Conservation Plan. The permit covers protected fish species and gives ODF Endangered Species Act certainty for covered management activities; the department says the remaining U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service permit for species including northern spotted owl, marbled murrelet, coastal marten and red tree vole is expected later this year or in early 2027.

The operational significance is predictability. ODF says the permit should reduce litigation exposure and make jobs and revenue from state forest management more predictable while habitat measures are implemented across the landscape.

Idaho — State Timber

Jackass Creek 2 puts 8,385 MBF on the block October 7

Idaho Department of Lands has advertised the Jackass Creek 2 sale in Kootenai County for October 7. The sale contains an estimated 8,385 MBF, carries an appraised net value of $1,526,727.50, requires a 10% bidder deposit, and starts at an average minimum of $220.53 per MBF. The contract term is four years.

The notice also warns that blowdown and insect- or disease-affected timber may add volume while reducing recovery. That combination makes the auction result useful not only as a stumpage signal but as a read on how buyers are pricing recovery risk in north Idaho.

Idaho — Salvage Timber

O’Donnell Darn Salvage adds another 2,270 MBF on September 23

The same state notice advertises O’Donnell Darn Salvage in Shoshone County for September 23, estimated at 2,270 MBF with a $171,435.50 appraised net value and a $90.78/MBF average starting minimum. The lower starting value and salvage character make the sale a useful companion signal to Jackass Creek 2.

Watching the two auctions together should help separate general buyer appetite from the discount buyers apply to damaged or uncertain recovery.

Markets & Pricing

North America — Softwood Lumber

Madison’s index edges back up to $525/MBF

Madison’s Lumber Prices Index for the week ending September 11 was $525 per thousand board feet, up $7, or about 1%, from $518 the previous week. The September 4 reading had itself been down slightly from the prior week, so the move is better read as stabilization than a breakout.

The useful near-term question is whether this post-Labor Day firming survives once normal buying patterns resume. A second or third weekly gain would carry more weight than one $7 move.

New Publications, Data & Tools

New review maps how westside PNW silviculture has changed

Journal of Forestry · Open access · Published September 11, 2026

A new review of contemporary silvicultural practice west of the Cascades finds three broad shifts: more emphasis on stand establishment and fewer intermediate treatments on intensive private ownerships; more variable-retention harvesting on state, BLM and tribal lands; and near-exclusive reliance on thinning on National Forest System lands.

The mill-side findings are particularly useful. The authors report that private lands now account for roughly 75% of annual removals while federal lands provide less than 10%; Douglas-fir makes up about 60–75% of annual cut; and only a handful of regional mills still buy logs larger than 60 cm on the small end. The paper argues that this processing shift is feeding back into rotation length, thinning decisions and other silvicultural choices.

Investment & M&A

FBS Business Lens — Timberland Optionality

Weyerhaeuser’s geothermal lease shows another way to monetize the land base

Hexagon Energy has leased geothermal rights on approximately 145,000 acres of Weyerhaeuser timberland in Oregon and Washington. The companies say the portfolio could eventually support more than 3 GW of generation, although sites remain in exploration and evaluation and no commercial locations have been finalized.

The FBS lesson is not geothermal specifically. It is asset optionality: a timberland owner can earn from the same underlying land base through timber, easements, access, energy rights, carbon, recreation or other rights without abandoning the core forest asset. Smaller owners will not reproduce a 145,000-acre geothermal deal, but the discipline is portable—periodically inventory every asset and right the business controls, not just the product it already sells.
