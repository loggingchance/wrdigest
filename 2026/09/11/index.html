Markets & Pricing

Global / North American Trade — Timber Supply

Rising wood demand is colliding with a new traceability burden

Reuters reports that global wood demand is projected to increase 37% by 2050 even as forest loss remains a major constraint. For North American producers, traders and pulp-and-paper suppliers, the near-term commercial issue is not only fibre availability but proof of origin: the EU Deforestation Regulation is scheduled to take effect at the end of 2026 and requires geolocation-based sourcing information for covered products.

The compliance market is already becoming a business in its own right. Timber traders and technology providers are building traceability services around EUDR requirements, and pulp and paper customers are among the users. That raises the value of clean chain-of-custody and origin data even for companies whose current sales are mostly domestic.

Government / Funding / Trade

California — Working Forests

Forest Legacy project-development funding is open through September 30

CAL FIRE opened its 2026 Forest Legacy capacity and project-development solicitation on September 8. The funding is intended to help eligible organizations prepare future working-forest conservation easement or fee-acquisition projects, including due diligence and other project-development work needed to compete for later state or federal Forest Legacy funding.

For land trusts, conservation organizations and partners working around productive timberland, this is useful because it funds the expensive pre-transaction work that often determines whether a conservation deal becomes financeable at all. The current application window closes September 30.

Market Opportunities

Pacific Northwest — Public Timber

Washington’s September auction book puts several million board feet on the market

Washington DNR’s September 22–24 auction cycle includes several sales already posted to the state calendar. Among them are the 2,337-MBF Q South Woody Mtn and 2,131-MBF Q Cold Park sales in Okanogan County, plus Northwest Region offerings including 972 MBF at Eye Bolt, 1,763 MBF at Take Two and 1,944 MBF at Prairie Mountain.

The advertised minimums range widely, which makes the eventual bidder count and premiums more informative than the appraisals themselves. Recent unsold Washington sales make this next round especially useful as a check on where buyer appetite is returning—or still absent.

Idaho — State Timber

Kootenai County sale offers about 7,923 tons on September 17

An Idaho Department of Lands sale advertised for September 17 covers an estimated 7,923 tons in Kootenai County. The posted appraised net sale value is $2,643.41, with an average minimum bid of $14.67 per ton and a three-year sale duration.

The notice says the sale may include blowdown or insect- and disease-affected timber, with corresponding recovery risk. That combination of low appraised value and uncertain recovery makes the bid result a useful small signal for how aggressively operators are pricing marginal material.

New Publications, Data & Tools

JoeScan pairs color vision with geometric scanning for mill optimization

Sawmill optimization · Timber Processing & Energy Expo · September 23–25, 2026

JoeScan plans to debut TrueSight, a coordinated system that combines geometric scan profiles with registered color images for defect detection, grading, cutting and sorting. The platform includes scan heads, color cameras, lighting, industrial computing, networking, calibration hardware and a simulation tool for integrators.

Of practical interest to existing mills, the company says transverse systems already using JS-50 WX scanners can be upgraded with the color-vision components rather than replaced outright. The system is scheduled for demonstration at TP&EE in Portland later this month.

Lake States — Equipment & Logging

Great Lakes Logging Expo continues today with more than 300 exhibitors

The 80th Great Lakes Logging & Heavy Equipment Expo is in its second and final day today at the Resch Expo in Green Bay. Organizers expect more than 300 exhibitors covering logging and construction equipment, trucks, firewood processors, portable sawmills, compact tractors and related suppliers.

Trade shows are imperfect market indicators, but concentrated dealer and contractor conversations can reveal what order books, used-equipment demand, financing appetite and replacement cycles look like before those changes show up in formal statistics.

FBS Business Lens — Practical AI

Finnos is treating agentic AI as an operating-system change, not a feature

Finnos, which develops scanning and optimization systems for sawmills, says it is reorganizing around agentic AI across internal processes, tools, enterprise systems and customer-facing products. A dedicated SaS unit is leading both internal adoption and new mill applications.

The useful small-business lesson is the architecture of the move: rather than asking where to add an AI button, the company is asking which recurring decisions and workflows can be delegated, accelerated or made more consistent. That is a better automation question for a mill, logging company or forestry consultancy than simply buying another standalone AI subscription.

Opportunities worth watching

  • EUDR traceability may create a premium for companies that can produce clean origin data without scrambling for it transaction by transaction. That is worth treating as an operations-data problem now, not merely an export-compliance problem later.
  • Washington’s September 22–24 auction results should be watched against the recent unsold-sale pattern. Bidder count and premiums will say more than advertised minimums about procurement confidence.
  • TP&EE later this month will provide a useful test of whether mills are buying incremental optimization upgrades—vision, scanning and software—or postponing capital spending amid uncertain lumber markets.
