Mills / Capacity / Procurement

Mountain West — Colorado

Montrose sawmill enters 60-day buyer search as contractor capacity starts leaking away

Neiman Enterprises has issued WARN notices to roughly 90 employees at Montrose Forest Products, Colorado's largest sawmill, with the 60-day period running through November 6. The company says the final outcome has not been determined and that it is trying to sell the operation as a going concern rather than simply close it.

The harder part may be rebuilding confidence around fibre and logging capacity. A buyer would need reliable, affordable long-term timber supply, while local reporting says only two of the eight independent logging contractors that had been working with the mill are still doing so; the others have already sought work elsewhere. All mill employees remain at work during the WARN period.

Government / Funding / Trade

United States — Hardwood Panels

USITC reaches final injury determination in hardwood and decorative plywood case

The U.S. International Trade Commission has determined that the U.S. industry is materially injured by imports of hardwood and decorative plywood from China, Indonesia and Vietnam that the Department of Commerce found to be subsidized and/or sold at less than fair value. The case excludes softwood structural plywood.

The determination moves the proceeding from investigation into the duty-order stage. For hardwood plywood importers, domestic panel producers, cabinet and furniture manufacturers, the practical issue now is landed cost and sourcing adjustment rather than whether the trade case will proceed.

Market Opportunities

Virginia — Government Timber

Fort Pickett sale opens bids today on 29,425 tons

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Fort Pickett sale covers 199.5 acres in nine blocks and contains an estimated 29,425 tons. The mix includes 9,510 tons of pine sawtimber, 1,473 tons of pine chip-n-saw, 8,057 tons of pine pulpwood, 1,735 tons of oak, poplar and miscellaneous hardwood sawtimber, 3,344 tons of hardwood pulpwood and 5,306 tons of chips.

Item 1 is a 103.2-acre clearcut; Item 2 combines a 14-acre clearcut with 80.5 acres of thinning. Bids open at 1 p.m. September 10. The mandatory pre-bid meeting has already passed, so today's value is primarily as a procurement and buyer-appetite signal rather than a new bidding opportunity.

British Columbia — Morice

Doray Creek offering closes on 25,010 m³, 94% balsam

British Columbia Timber Sales' Doray Creek offering in the Morice area is advertised at 25,010 cubic metres over 77.8 hectares, with the volume estimated at 94% balsam and 6% spruce. The upset stumpage rate is $21.97 per cubic metre and the advertised term is 23 months.

Bids close September 10. The eventual result, if published, will be more useful than the upset rate itself: balsam-heavy sales can provide a clean local read on appetite for lower-value interior fibre and on how much competition remains in a constrained B.C. operating environment.

New Publications, Data & Tools

National road erosion and mass-wasting risk data

U.S. Forest Service Rocky Mountain Research Station · September 9, 2026 · National / forest roads & watershed planning

The Forest Service has released what it describes as the first dataset showing erosion impacts across all Forest Service roads. The national layer gives managers a rapid way to screen road-related erosion and mass-wasting risk when planning timber, fuels, watershed and access work rather than beginning every analysis from a blank map.

The agency says watershed specialists in Oregon, Washington and California are already using the data and saving many hours of analysis. That makes this less a research curiosity than a potentially useful pre-project triage tool for deciding where detailed field review deserves attention first.

Under the Radar

Industrial Wood Packaging — Design Software

Crate Design System brings an engineering workflow to wood shipping crates

Crate Design System has launched as dedicated software for designing sheathed and cleated crates and basic lumber box crates. The program can generate detailed designs, three-dimensional and exploded views, and standardized outputs using a workflow derived from the same general design-system approach long used in pallets.

The first release is still prescriptive rather than a full structural-analysis engine, which the developers describe as a future step. Even so, this is the kind of small ecosystem development worth noticing: better design software can reduce overbuilding, improve quoting consistency and move industrial-wood packaging toward more repeatable engineering rather than shop rules of thumb.

Opportunities worth watching

  • Montrose is now a race between transaction timing and supply-chain erosion. A buyer needs not only timber access but enough logging and trucking capacity left in the basin to restart without rebuilding the contractor network from scratch.
  • Watch the Fort Pickett and Doray Creek bid results rather than their advertised values. Bidder count and premiums over minimums will provide better local market information than the sale notices themselves.
  • The Forest Service road-risk dataset could become a useful front-end layer for timber-sale layout, fuels planning and watershed review. The next question is how easily practitioners can move the data into ordinary GIS workflows and field prioritization.
