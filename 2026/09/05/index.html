What matters this morning

  • Washington State has accelerated salvage-sale authority after record fire damage to state trust forests. The Board of Natural Resources raised the value of salvage sales DNR can auction without board approval from $250,000 to $2 million, cutting roughly two months from the process.
  • A new analysis of the Coosa Pines, Alabama pulp-mill closure puts numbers on what loss of a low-grade wood market can do. Pulpwood stumpage reportedly fell from about $7/ton to effectively zero within an 85-mile radius after the mill and associated chip facility stopped buying.

Forestry & Timber

Pacific Northwest

Washington speeds salvage sales after record state-land fire losses

Washington DNR says 93,000 acres of state trust land have burned this season, about two-thirds of it forest. The Board of Natural Resources this week raised the threshold for salvage sales that can proceed without board approval from $250,000 to $2 million. DNR estimates it may be able to salvage only about 4,000 of roughly 60,000 burned forest acres, and officials expect strong competition from burned timber on tribal, private and federal land. On the Sinlahekin fire alone, two future sales that might have brought at least $800,000 now carry combined salvage appraisals of about $218,000.

Inland West

Idaho sale offers 56,044 tons at a $1.26 million appraised value

Idaho Department of Lands will auction the Berried Butte GNA sale in Kootenai County on September 9. The sale is estimated at 56,044 tons, with an appraised net value of $1,256,949 and an average minimum bid of $27.96/ton. The required bid security is $125,694.92. It is a useful live data point on state timber-sale scale and minimum pricing in northern Idaho.

Mills / Capacity / Procurement

Sierra Nevada

Tahoe Forest Products secures $50 million in USDA-backed financing

Tahoe Forest Products, operating in Carson City, has secured $50 million in USDA-backed loans to scale up a sawmill built around material coming from Sierra Nevada forest-management work. Local reporting describes it as the first significant new sawmilling capacity in the region in decades. The significance is less the headline lumber volume than the restoration outlet: additional local processing capacity can change the economics of thinning and fuels work where haul distance has been a persistent constraint.

Markets & Pricing

U.S. South

Coosa Pines closure shows the local cost of losing a pulpwood outlet

A newly reported economic analysis of Domtar’s Coosa Pines closure estimates nearly $7 billion in regional losses over a decade. The forestry signal is more immediate: the analysis says pine pulpwood stumpage in an approximately 85-mile procurement radius fell from around $7/ton to zero after the mill closed. Whether every assumption in the ten-year estimate holds or not, the observed market shock is a stark measure of how little optionality landowners and loggers have when a large low-grade outlet disappears.

Madison’s index slips to $518/MBF

Madison’s Lumber Prices Index for the week ending September 4 was US$518/MBF, down $3, or about 1%, from the prior week. The move is modest, but it extends the soft tone visible at the end of August rather than showing a post-summer-break rebound.

Market Opportunities

California

French Meadows seeks removal and delivery of 2,520 green tons

Placer County is seeking a contractor to load, process, haul and deliver about 2,520 green tons of deteriorated whole-log biomass from the French Meadows restoration area on Tahoe National Forest to the Rio Bravo–Rocklin biomass facility. The mandatory field walk occurred September 4; bids are due September 17. The solicitation is a concrete example of public restoration work carrying an identified biomass destination rather than leaving disposal entirely to the contractor.

Operations Technology

A timber-hauling software startup is targeting the scale-ticket problem

Neurobird has opened early access for a purpose-built log-hauling platform aimed at landing load records, scale-ticket matching, missing-ticket alerts, tract/product tracking, haul-rate sheets and settlement runs. It is still explicitly in development, so this is not a product endorsement. But it is worth watching because the feature set is aimed at the ugly, expensive administrative gap between the landing, the truck and mill settlement rather than at generic fleet telematics.

Government / Funding / Trade

EPA and Army reopen part of the WOTUS rulemaking

EPA and the Army signed a supplemental proposed rule on September 4 that adds regulatory alternatives to the 2025 WOTUS proposal. A 30-day comment period will begin when it is published in the Federal Register. For forestry, the eventual definition remains important to road building, crossings, drainage work, harvesting BMP implementation and Clean Water Act permitting boundaries.

EPA WOTUS materials →

DOE opens up to $58 million for alternative and waste feedstock chemistry

DOE’s Alternative Fuels and Feedstocks Office announced up to $58 million on September 4 for R&D and pre-pilot chemical technologies using domestic alternative and waste feedstocks. The announcement is broader than forestry, but explicitly includes biomass and may be relevant to firms developing higher-value uses for forest residuals, mill residues and other low-value woody feedstocks.

Opportunities worth watching

  • Washington’s salvage acceleration could create a short, concentrated supply pulse into a market already facing burned timber from other ownerships. Watch auction premiums, species mix and haul distances rather than just announced acreage.
  • The Coosa Pines numbers make replacement low-grade markets in the Southeast more than a development-policy talking point. Where pulpwood is effectively non-merchantable, even modest new demand can have an outsized effect on thinning economics and contractor utilization.
  • Tahoe Forest Products and the French Meadows biomass work are worth viewing together: one adds local solid-wood processing while the other routes deteriorated material to energy. The Sierra restoration bottleneck is increasingly about assembling enough outlets for different material classes, not finding one magic facility.
