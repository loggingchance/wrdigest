Pulp, Paper & Biomass

Canada — New Brunswick

Nackawic plans end-October idle of 190,000-tonne dissolving-pulp mill

AV Group NB says it will temporarily idle its Nackawic pulp operation around the end of October after assessing weak market conditions and broader macroeconomic pressure. Grasim Industries, which owns 45% of AV Group NB, puts the mill's dissolving-grade pulp capacity at approximately 190,000 tonnes per year; the company's two New Brunswick mills together total about 332,000 tonnes.

Local reporting says roughly 350 workers are affected and notes that the mill had already stopped buying wood in August as an inventory adjustment. For the regional woods side, that earlier procurement stop matters as much as the October production date: a major fibre outlet has already been pulling less wood.

Forestry & Timber

West — Washington

Three unsold DNR sales roll into next week's auction cycle

Washington DNR's current unsold list shows Q Parody Peak in Stevens County at 4,960 MBF and a $1.28 million minimum, Country Boy in Cowlitz County at 5,238 MBF and $870,000, and Q Dead End in Yakima County at 2,979 MBF and $38,000. These sales received no bids at their initial auctions and can generally be bid at minimum appraised value during the next scheduled regional auction.

That makes next week's September 22–24 DNR auction cycle worth watching for more than the new offerings. Reoffers provide a clean read on where appraised value, operating conditions and current mill demand are failing to meet.

West — Washington

DNR has two September 18 fuels-reduction contracts in the pipeline

Washington DNR's silviculture-contract page lists a Good Neighbor Authority hazardous-fuels reduction contract and a Southeast Region mechanical-fuels reduction contract with September 18 dates. The GNA work occurs on federal land and may be administered jointly by DNR and Forest Service staff.

These solicitations are useful reminders that the public-lands work pipeline is not limited to timber sales. Mechanical fuels work and stand-treatment contracts can be meaningful adjacent markets for contractors with the right equipment and operating geography.

Under the Radar

Northeast — Pennsylvania

Yoder's Timber Harvesting liquidates complete operating lineup

Yoder's Timber Harvesting in Belleville, Pennsylvania held an absolute liquidation Friday covering logging equipment, trucks, trailers, skid steers and shop equipment. The auction description says owner Rudy Yoder is moving in another direction after 17 years in logging.

One liquidation is not an industry trend, but complete contractor exits matter locally because machines can change hands faster than experienced operating capacity can be replaced. The sale is worth noting alongside the broader concern about contractor availability in several wood baskets.

Industry Ecosystem & Service Providers

Lake States — Minnesota

North Star Expo puts the logging supply chain in one place today

The Minnesota Timber Producers Association's 72nd North Star Expo continues today at the Itasca County Fairgrounds in Grand Rapids. The event is built around loggers, truckers, agency personnel and equipment/service suppliers, with roughly 200 students also expected.

For an industry concerned about both contractor capacity and workforce replacement, the student component is more than an event detail: it puts potential entrants in direct contact with the machines, firms and people that make the regional supply chain work.

Opportunities worth watching

  • Nackawic's wood flow before the October idle: watch where suppliers redirect fibre and whether Atholville absorbs any volume.
  • Washington's September 22–24 auctions: watch whether the three reoffered sales clear at minimum value or remain stranded.
  • Secondary-market logging equipment from the Yoder liquidation: local machine transfers can preserve equipment capacity even when an operating business exits.
