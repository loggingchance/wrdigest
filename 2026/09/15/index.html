Pulp, Paper & Biomass

Canada — Quebec / Northeastern Ontario

Témiscaming shutdown takes effect today

RYAM’s indefinite suspension at its Témiscaming complex takes effect September 15. Regional reporting puts the direct impact at roughly 400 unionized workers, with contractors, suppliers, transportation firms and businesses across the Quebec–Ontario border also exposed.

The facility produces pulp and coated paperboard. For the wood side of the business, the immediate question is how long the outage lasts and where fibre that would have moved through Témiscaming can economically be redirected.

Market Opportunities

Inland West — Idaho

BLM Top Hat sale reaches the final eight days

BLM’s Cottonwood Field Office is accepting bids on the Top Hat timber sale through September 23 at 4:30 p.m. Pacific, with the sale scheduled for the same day. The sale notice was posted August 25 and the agency has the prospectus and sale map available online.

For buyers working the Idaho market, this is the next near-term federal offering worth putting against state and other federal procurement opportunities as September auctions bunch together.

Under the Radar

Southeast — North Carolina · FBS business lens

TimberClear tries to make a fragmented timber market searchable

TimberClear currently lists 247 timber-industry entries across 25 North Carolina towns, including 82 timber buyers and 154 consulting foresters, alongside independent loggers and mills. It also includes a basic timber-value calculator and is explicitly starting with North Carolina.

The business lesson is transaction friction: small timber sales often have a search problem before they have a price problem. A directory that makes buyers, foresters and contractors easier to find could improve market participation without changing the underlying timber resource at all.

Markets & Pricing

Pacific Northwest — Oregon

Forest Products Forum puts current market calls on one stage today

The Forest Products Forum opens September 15 at World Forestry Center in Portland ahead of CANOPY 2026. The program is built around current assessments of macroeconomics and housing, lumber, timber, trade, engineered lumber and wood panels.

The value is less the conference itself than the concentration of market views: this week should produce a useful read on where analysts see the largest gaps between timber supply, mill economics and end-product demand.

Hardwoods — North America

Hardwood supply chain gathers in Cleveland tomorrow

The National Hardwood Lumber Association Annual Convention runs September 16–18 in Cleveland. NHLA describes the audience as sawmill owners, lumber buyers, manufacturers, suppliers and wood-products professionals, with coverage spanning grading, kiln drying, exporting, manufacturing and sales.

With hardwood markets often more fragmented than structural softwood markets, the useful signals will be buyer sentiment, kiln and inventory conditions, export expectations and equipment spending—not simply attendance.

Opportunities worth watching

  • Témiscaming fibre displacement: watch for alternate procurement outlets, contractor impacts and any indication of shutdown duration.
  • BLM’s September 23 Top Hat sale: the bid result will add another useful data point to a busy western September auction calendar.
